What We Do

From Static Models to Living Digital Twins

Sentra's Digital Twin solutions bridge the gap between design, construction, and operations by creating dynamic, data-enriched virtual replicas that evolve with your assets in real time.

Intelligent Virtual Replicas for Real-World Assets

A Digital Twin is more than a 3D model; it is a continuously updated digital mirror of a physical asset, building, infrastructure system, or operational process. Unlike static documentation or conventional BIM, a Digital Twin integrates real-world data from IoT sensors, connected systems, and operational records to provide continuous, real-time visibility into asset performance and condition throughout its lifecycle.

Sentra builds the twin from what is actually on site. Drone survey, terrestrial LiDAR, handheld SLAM and 360° imagery capture the asset as it stands; the point cloud is modelled into named components; and inspection records, maintenance points and construction history are attached to the components they describe. We have delivered this for a 40.23 m open web girder railway bridge for South Central Railway and for TGNPDCL's Bathupally substation. Because we also install structural monitoring, live sensor channels are bound to the same components in our in-house monitoring application, so readings and alerts appear on the part of the structure they come from.

  • Built from measured reality capture, not old drawings
  • Component-level model with records attached
  • Georeferenced measurement without a site visit
  • Live monitoring data bound to components

Process

How It Works

A structured, scalable process from data capture through to live Digital Twin deployment.

01 Step One

Data Capture & 3D Scanning

Terrestrial LiDAR scanning, drone photogrammetry, and BIM model audit capture complete as-built geometry of existing assets or design models for new builds.

02 Step Two

Digital Twin Platform Setup

The BIM model is integrated into our Digital Twin platform with all asset data, attributes, and relationships classified to ISO 19650 and ready for operational data connection.

03 Step Three

IoT Sensor Integration

Live data streams from IoT sensors, including structural, environmental, energy, and operational, are connected to the Digital Twin, enabling real-time visualisation and analytics.

04 Step Four

Analytics & Dashboard Configuration

Alert thresholds, predictive models, and custom dashboards are configured to visualise key performance indicators, operational metrics, and condition data in the 3D model context.

05 Step Five

Validation & User Training

The Digital Twin is validated against physical asset data, user access controls are configured, and operations and maintenance teams are trained on the platform.

06 Step Six

Continuous Evolution

The Digital Twin evolves with the asset, integrating new sensor data, inspection records, maintenance events, and modifications to keep the virtual replica permanently synchronised with physical reality.

Benefits

How Digital Twins Help Your Organisation

Digital Twins create value at every stage of the asset lifecycle, from design coordination through 30-year operational management.

Lifecycle Continuity

Bridge the gap between design, construction, and operations by reducing information loss at handover and ensuring that data created during delivery remains accessible and usable throughout the asset life.

Proactive Control

Maintenance findings are logged against the component they refer to, so open issues, repeat problems and inspection coverage can be read off the model instead of reconstructed from registers.

Operational Efficiency

Optimise resource utilisation, energy consumption, space allocation, and maintenance coordination by reducing operational costs while improving service levels.

Evidence-Based Decisions

Data-driven insights for maintenance planning, capital investment, and operational strategy by replacing intuition and anecdotal evidence with verifiable, real-time data.

Improved Safety

Monitor environmental conditions, structural health, and safety systems in real time, with automated alerts that enable rapid response to emerging risks.

Collaborative Working

Enable design teams, contractors, facility managers, and asset owners to collaborate on the same current digital representation by reducing coordination errors, travel costs, and decision latency.

Structured Asset Information

Each structure, piece of equipment and civil element is a separate, identifiable object, which is what lets inspection records, measurements and later sensor data attach to the right asset.

Featured Project

Bathupally Substation, TGNPDCL

A browser-based asset twin of an outdoor distribution substation: 360° imagery, point cloud and a component-level 3D model in one georeferenced viewer.

Telangana Northern Power Distribution Company Limited wanted the Bathupally substation documented in a form its engineers could use from a desk. The yard was captured as it stands and modelled asset by asset, so every transformer, gantry, isolator, kiosk and cable trench exists as a separate object with real coordinates.

What the twin does today:
  • Remote walk-through of every bay in 360° imagery
  • Point, distance and height measurement in real units
  • Maintenance points of interest pinned to equipment
  • Construction footage linked to the assets it shows

Condition sensors are not yet connected; the model gives each future sensor an asset to report against. Read the case study. For city-scale work, see Clove Technologies' Amaravati command and control centre.

Buyer Guide

Planning an Infrastructure Digital Twin

What goes into a monitoring-grade digital twin, how long it takes, and what to check before you commission one.

What goes into it

  • Reality capture: drone survey, terrestrial LiDAR, handheld SLAM and 360° imagery
  • A model of named components built from the point cloud
  • Inspection, maintenance and construction records attached to those components
  • Live sensor channels bound to the part of the structure they measure

How it is delivered

Data capture and scanning come first, followed by platform setup with data classified to ISO 19650. Then we integrate the IoT sensors, configure analytics and dashboards, validate the twin and train your team. After that, the twin keeps evolving with the asset.

What drives the cost

There is no fixed price; scope is set by the structure and the programme. The main drivers are:

  • Size of the asset and the scanning effort
  • Level of model detail and number of components
  • Number of live sensor channels to bind
  • Existing systems to integrate (BMS, SCADA, CMMS)
  • Number of users and training required

How long it takes

A single building or infrastructure asset typically takes 8–16 weeks from survey to live platform. That breaks down as: scanning 1–2 days, BIM model 2–4 weeks, sensor installation 1–2 weeks, configuration and integration 2–4 weeks, and acceptance testing and training 1 week.

Platforms and protocols

Delivered twins run in a browser-based viewer that combines 360° imagery, point cloud and the 3D model with measurement and records. Monitoring hardware reports over LoRaWAN, 4G and Modbus RTU. If you already run a twin or asset platform, we confirm what we can deliver into it before quoting.

A BIM model is a static digital representation of an asset, capturing geometry, attributes, and relationships as they existed at a point in time. A Digital Twin is a BIM model connected to live data streams from IoT sensors, maintenance systems, and operational records by creating a continuously updated digital representation that mirrors the current real-world state of the physical asset. The Digital Twin enables real-time performance monitoring, anomaly detection, and predictive analytics in the spatial context of the 3D model.

A Digital Twin ingests data from multiple sources: BIM models, IoT sensors (structural, environmental, energy), operational systems (BMS, SCADA, CMMS), and manual inspection records, into a single, accessible environment. The platform processes, visualises, and analyses this data within the spatial context of the 3D model. Users can view real-time conditions, run simulations, receive alerts on anomalies, and generate reports, all from the same interface.

Key benefits include: reduced downtime through real-time anomaly detection and predictive maintenance; improved operational efficiency via optimised resource utilisation and energy management; better capital planning through accurate asset health and lifecycle data; enhanced safety through continuous environmental and structural monitoring; and improved collaboration between design, construction, and operations teams working from a single source of truth.
