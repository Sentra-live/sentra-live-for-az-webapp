A distribution substation is one of the densest places a utility owns. Power transformers, isolators, circuit breakers, current and potential transformers, lightning arresters, control kiosks, cable trenches and overhead gantries all share a fenced yard, and most of it is energised. Knowing exactly what is where, what condition it is in and what was done to it last usually means either a site visit or a search through registers and drawings that may no longer match the yard.

Telangana Northern Power Distribution Company Limited (TGNPDCL) wanted the Bathupally substation documented in a way its engineers could use from a desk. Clove Technologies delivered that as a [digital twin](https://sentratech.in/solutions/digital-twin.html): the substation captured as it stands, modelled asset by asset, and published in a web viewer where maintenance teams can walk the yard, measure equipment and log issues against real coordinates.

## Why Substations Are Hard to Manage on Paper

Most substation records are organised by document, not by location. A transformer's test history lives in one register, the single line diagram in another file, and the photographs from the last inspection somewhere on a shared drive. None of it tells an engineer where on the yard a problem actually is, or what the equipment around it looks like today.

Four problems came up repeatedly:

- As-built drawings drift from reality as equipment is added, replaced or relocated over the life of the substation.

- Simple questions such as a clearance, a structure height or which bay a breaker sits in often need someone to travel to site and check.

- Energised equipment limits how close anyone can get, so measuring by hand is slow and needs safety clearances.

- Maintenance observations are written up as text. The next person has to work out which piece of equipment the note refers to.

A digital twin solves these by putting the record and the location in the same place. Every observation, measurement and document is tied to a point in a model of the actual substation.

## What We Built

01. Reality capture of the yard

The substation was captured as it stands. 360° panoramic imagery was recorded at stations across the switchyard, giving a photographic walk-through of every bay, and an aerial point cloud of the full site provides the overhead reference. In the viewer the point cloud appears as a minimap in the corner, so a user always knows where they are standing in the yard.

![360 degree panoramic view of the Bathupally substation switchyard with power transformer, gantries and minimap](https://sentratech.in/image/case-studies/substation-digital-asset-management-predictive-maintenance/360-panorama-view.webp)

The 360° imagery layer. The power transformer sits centre frame with the incoming gantries behind it, and the point cloud minimap at the bottom right shows the viewer's position on the site.

02. A 3D model of every asset

From the captured data, the substation was modelled component by component. Gantry columns and beams, insulator strings, the power transformer and its conservator, isolators, kiosks, plinths, cable trenches and the boundary fence each exist as separate objects. The model shares the same coordinates as the imagery, so a user can switch between the photograph and the model at any position and see the same equipment.

![3D asset model of the Bathupally substation showing transformer, gantry structures, conductors and plinths](https://sentratech.in/image/case-studies/substation-digital-asset-management-predictive-maintenance/3d-model-view.webp)

The same view in the 3D model layer. Structures, conductors, the transformer and civil works are separate objects, which is what allows records to be attached to individual assets.

This is the step that turns a survey into an asset register. A photograph shows a transformer; a model knows it is a transformer, where it stands and what surrounds it. Modelling from captured data is the core of our [digital engineering and documentation](https://sentratech.in/solutions/digital-engineering-and-documentation.html) work.

03. Measurement without a site visit

Because the model and imagery are georeferenced, the viewer measures in real units. Engineers can take point coordinates, straight-line distances and vertical heights by clicking two points. A gantry column, for example, measures 7.86 m from plinth to beam in the screenshot below. Clearances, bay spacing and equipment dimensions can be checked the same way, without a permit to work or a tape measure near live conductors.

![Measurement tool in the substation digital twin showing a 7.86 metre gantry column height](https://sentratech.in/image/case-studies/substation-digital-asset-management-predictive-maintenance/measurement-tool.webp)

The measurement toolbar offers point, distance and height modes. Here a gantry column is measured at 7.86 m directly in the 3D model.

04. Points of interest for maintenance

Maintenance findings are logged as points of interest (POIs) placed directly on the equipment. Each POI carries a category such as _Maintenance Required_, the asset it belongs to, its X, Y and Z coordinates, and a free-text description. The next engineer to open the model sees the flag on the transformer itself rather than a line in a spreadsheet.

![Creating a Maintenance Required point of interest on the transformer with X, Y and Z coordinates](https://sentratech.in/image/case-studies/substation-digital-asset-management-predictive-maintenance/point-of-interest.webp)

Creating a POI on the transformer. The category, asset and coordinates are recorded with the note, so the issue is always found in the same place.

Over time these POIs build into a maintenance history organised by location. Which transformer has the most open issues, which bay was last inspected, and which findings are still unresolved all become questions the model can answer.

05. Construction records linked to the site

The twin also holds the substation's construction history. In construction mode, video recorded while the gantries, plinths and transformer were being installed plays over the model. When a question comes up later about how a foundation was built or what sits under a trench cover, the footage is one click away and already linked to the right place.

![Construction phase video of the substation gantries playing inside the digital twin viewer](https://sentratech.in/image/case-studies/substation-digital-asset-management-predictive-maintenance/construction-video.webp)

Construction mode plays site footage from the build phase inside the viewer, with the 33 kV gantries being erected and the transformer being installed.

## From Asset Register to Predictive Maintenance

The twin as delivered is a digital asset management system: it records what is on the site, where it is and what has been done to it. Predictive maintenance is what comes next, and the model is what makes it practical.

Predictive maintenance needs condition data tied to specific assets. For a substation that means sensors on the equipment most likely to fail and most expensive to replace: vibration and temperature on transformers, tilt on gantry structures, and settlement on plinths and foundations. With the twin already in place, each sensor has a location and an asset to report against, and an alert opens on the equipment it refers to rather than as an anonymous tag number.

This is the same approach we describe in [digital twins for predictive maintenance](https://sentratech.in/article/digital-twin-predictive-maintenance-sentra.html), and Sentra's [wireless edge devices](https://sentratech.in/products/edge-devices.html) such as the [vibration meter](https://sentratech.in/products/vibration-meter.html) and [tiltmeter](https://sentratech.in/products/tiltmeter.html) are built for this kind of retrofit, with no cabling across an energised yard. For rotating and electrical plant more broadly, see our article on [IoT vibration sensors for predictive maintenance](https://sentratech.in/article/predictive-maintenance-industrial-facilities-iot-vibration-sensors.html).

## What the Project Delivered

TGNPDCL now holds a measured, navigable digital record of the Bathupally substation. In practice the twin supports:

- Remote walk-throughs of the switchyard for planning and review

- A 3D asset register with each structure and piece of equipment modelled separately

- Measurements of heights, distances and clearances without entering the yard

- Maintenance issues logged as geo-tagged POIs on the equipment they concern

- Construction footage kept with the asset for future reference

- A spatial base for adding condition monitoring sensors and moving to predictive maintenance

## Conclusion

The Bathupally project shows what a substation looks like when its records are organised by location instead of by file. Imagery, model, measurements, maintenance notes and construction history sit in one viewer, and every item in it points to a real place on the yard.

For a distribution utility with hundreds of substations, that is the starting point for managing them as a portfolio. Add [asset monitoring](https://sentratech.in/solutions/asset-monitoring-and-management-solutions.html) and the same twin moves from recording condition to predicting it.
