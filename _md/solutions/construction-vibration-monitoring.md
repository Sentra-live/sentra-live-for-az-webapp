## Why Construction Vibration Monitoring Matters

Construction vibration monitoring for piling, demolition, tunnelling and blasting: wireless sensors on adjacent buildings, PPV alerts and automated compliance reports.

### Construction Vibration Monitoring

Construction sites often sit metres from heritage buildings, hospitals, data centres and live transport infrastructure. Vibration from the works travels through the ground into neighbouring structures and can cause cosmetic damage, structural cracking or worse.

Continuous monitoring gives you instant alerts when a threshold is exceeded, and a documented record to use as evidence if a damage claim arises. It also lets you adjust methods and equipment to reduce vibration without halting work.

## Typical PPV Limits

Most jurisdictions set vibration limits as peak particle velocity in mm/s. The limits for your project come from the applicable standard and contract; these are common ranges.

2–5 mm/s Heritage and sensitive buildings

5–10 mm/s Residential buildings

10–25 mm/s Commercial and industrial structures

15–50 mm/s Buried utilities and pipelines, by material and condition

## Setting Up Vibration Monitoring Around Your Site

How a construction vibration programme is planned, what it costs to run, and what it gives you if a damage claim is raised.

### How it is deployed

We identify the nearest receptors and the transmission path from the works. Sensors go at foundation level and at several elevations on tall buildings, with reference sensors away from the site. Then we record an ambient baseline and start live monitoring, with FFT analysis at the sensor node.

### Know your vibration sources

- Piling: high-energy, low-frequency, travels far

- Demolition: impulsive, high-amplitude

- Tunnelling: continuous, low-level, with settlement risk

- Compaction: sustained surface vibration

- Blasting: transient, high-frequency

### What drives the cost

There is no fixed price; scope is set by the structure and the programme. The main drivers are:

- Number of adjacent structures and sensors

- Duration of the works

- Tilt and settlement alongside vibration

- Alert levels and response arrangements

- Reporting cadence for stakeholders and regulators

### Alerts and reports

Thresholds are set per sensor, for PPV, tilt, displacement or rate of change, and breaches notify the project team immediately. Reports are generated automatically, daily, weekly or per event.

### Evidence if a claim is raised

A continuous, timestamped vibration record documents what neighbouring structures were actually exposed to. That gives you evidence in damage claims or disputes, and shows proactive care of the surrounding buildings.

##

Continuous measurement of ground-borne vibration from activities such as piling, demolition, tunnelling, compaction and blasting, at the structures around the site. Readings are compared against agreed peak particle velocity limits, and the project team is alerted as a limit is approached.

##

Limits are usually set as peak particle velocity (PPV) in mm/s by the applicable standard and contract. Common ranges are 2–5 mm/s for heritage and sensitive buildings, 5–10 mm/s for residential, 10–25 mm/s for commercial and industrial structures, and 15–50 mm/s for buried utilities depending on material and condition.

##

At foundation level of adjacent structures, where ground-borne vibration enters the building. Taller buildings need sensors at several elevations to capture amplification. Place sensors along the transmission path between the works and the nearest receptors, plus reference sensors away from the site to record ambient levels.

##

Alerts are configurable per sensor: a PPV limit, a tilt or displacement threshold, or a rate of change that suggests accelerating movement all generate immediate notifications to the project team. Reports can be generated daily, weekly or per event for stakeholders and regulators.
