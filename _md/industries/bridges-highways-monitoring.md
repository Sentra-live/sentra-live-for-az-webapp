## Why Bridge
Monitoring Matters

Bridges and highway structures face continuous stress from traffic loads, environmental exposure, and aging materials. Cracks, corrosion, and fatigue develop over time, often invisible until they become critical.

## Choosing a Bridge Monitoring System

The questions bridge owners and engineers ask before commissioning continuous monitoring, answered from our own deployments.

### What it monitors

- Deflection and displacement of spans

- Strain on girders, decks and expansion joints

- Pier tilt and bearing rotation

- Vibration frequency and amplitude under traffic

- Crack width, and fatigue cycle counting for remaining-life assessment

### How it is installed

After a site survey, strain gauges go on girders and deck sections, tiltmeters on piers and bearings, and accelerometers capture dynamic response. Sensors are retrofitted on live structures and report through LoRaWAN or cellular gateways to one dashboard.

### What drives the cost

There is no fixed price; scope is set by the structure and the programme. The main drivers are:

- Number of spans, piers and bearings instrumented

- Trend monitoring only, or dynamic response as well

- Access: traffic management, height, water crossings

- Whether load testing or fatigue assessment is included

- Monitoring duration and reporting cadence

[How monitoring cost is built up](https://sentratech.in/blogs/structural-health-monitoring-cost-factors.html)

### How fast you see results

Sensors report periodically, every few minutes to hours, and immediately when a threshold is crossed. Readings start within hours to days of installation, and a reliable baseline typically takes 2–6 weeks. We recommend a 4–8 week pilot.

### Alerts your team can act on

Thresholds are set against structural design limits, so each level has an agreed response: warning, alert and critical. Notifications go out by SMS, email and dashboard, and scheduled engineering reports record what was measured and why it matters.

### Bridges we have monitored

We monitored a four-lane highway bridge, more than 30 years old and carrying about 55,000 vehicles a day, for a year. We also instrumented a 1960s steel railway bridge under running trains. ## Frequently Asked Questions

##

A bridge health monitoring system is a network of sensors, typically strain gauges, tiltmeters and accelerometers, that continuously track how a bridge deforms, vibrates and settles under live and environmental loads, giving engineers real, trended data instead of relying only on periodic visual inspection.

##

A typical deployment combines strain gauges on girders and deck sections, tiltmeters on piers and bearings, and accelerometers for dynamic response, all reporting through LoRaWAN or cellular gateways into a single dashboard.

##

Sentra's sensors support both periodic reporting, commonly every few minutes to hours for long-term trend tracking, and triggered event detection that reports immediately when movement, strain or vibration crosses a defined threshold.

##

It depends on the bridge and the programme: how many spans, piers and bearings are instrumented, whether dynamic response is measured as well as long-term trends, access conditions such as traffic, height or water, whether load testing or fatigue assessment is included, and how long monitoring runs. A site survey and a short pilot are the usual way to fix scope and budget. Request a monitoring assessment and we will scope it for your bridge.
