# Metro Rail Monitoring: Tunnels, Viaducts and Stations in Real Time

> What to monitor on a metro line: tunnel settlement and convergence, viaduct piers and bearings, station excavations and vibration, in build and service.

Published: 2026-10-16  
Source: https://sentratech.in/article/metro-rail-monitoring-tunnels-viaducts-stations.html

Bored tunnels, deep station boxes and kilometres of elevated viaduct each move, wear and fail differently, and none of them is easy to reach once trains are running. Here is what real-time monitoring tracks on each, during construction and in service.

A metro line is three kinds of structure stitched together: bored tunnels under the city, deep excavations for underground stations, and kilometres of elevated viaduct on piers above the traffic. Each behaves differently, fails differently and is hard to get at once trains are running. Real-time monitoring is how metro owners and contractors keep track of all three, first while the line is being built in a crowded city, and then for the decades it carries passengers.

## Two Phases, Two Sets of Questions

Monitoring on a metro project answers different questions at different times:

- **During construction,** the question is mostly about the neighbours. Is tunnelling or excavation moving the ground, tilting the buildings above, or shaking heritage structures and utilities beyond agreed limits?

- **In operation,** the question turns to the asset itself. Is the tunnel lining deforming, are viaduct piers and bearings behaving as designed under thousands of daily train passages, and where should the next maintenance block be spent?

The same sensor network can serve both. Instruments installed for construction safety leave a baseline that makes operational monitoring far more meaningful.

## Tunnels: Settlement, Convergence and the Buildings Above

When a tunnel boring machine passes under a city, the ground above settles into a shallow trough. Monitoring tracks that trough and its effect on everything sitting in it:

- **Buildings and structures above the alignment:** [tiltmeters](https://sentratech.in/products/tiltmeter.html) on facades and columns show rotation as the machine approaches and passes, and crack gauges track existing cracks.

- **Ground movement:** surface settlement points and subsurface [inclinometers](https://sentratech.in/blogs/what-is-an-inclinometer-digital-tiltmeter-guide.html) show how far the movement spreads.

- **The tunnel itself:** convergence, the change in distance across the tunnel, shows whether the lining is squeezing or ovalising. A [laser tiltmeter](https://sentratech.in/products/laser-tiltmeter.html) measures distance to a fixed target across the tunnel together with 3-axis tilt, with no contact with the moving surface.

In operation the same measurements continue at lower frequency, joined by checks on joint opening and water ingress. Underground, connectivity needs planning: wireless sensors in tunnels report over LoRaWAN through [repeaters](https://sentratech.in/products/repeater.html) that carry the signal along the bore to a [gateway](https://sentratech.in/products/gateway.html) at the portal or shaft.

## Viaducts: Piers, Bearings and Dynamic Response

Elevated sections are bridges, and they are monitored like bridges, with one difference: the load arrives every few minutes, all day. What matters on a viaduct:

- **Pier and foundation behaviour:** tiltmeters on piers pick up rotation or settlement, the earliest sign of a foundation problem, especially where piers stand near new excavations or drainage channels.

- **Bearings and expansion joints:** displacement and tilt at bearings show whether the deck is moving freely with temperature or has started to bind.

- **Dynamic response:** [accelerometers](https://sentratech.in/products/accelerometers.html) measure natural frequency and damping. A falling natural frequency, once corrected for temperature, signals lost stiffness. Bridge modal work typically samples at 100 to 200 Hz.

- **Stress at critical details:** [strain gauges](https://sentratech.in/products/strain-gauges.html) sampled at 50 to 100 Hz under traffic give the stress-range history that fatigue assessment needs.

Temperature is logged at each sensor location, so daily thermal movement is not mistaken for structural change. ## Stations and Deep Excavations

Underground stations are usually built as deep excavations held up by diaphragm walls and struts, often right next to existing buildings. The excavation is the riskiest phase of the whole project, and it is watched closely:

- Wall deflection, measured with inclinometers built into the diaphragm walls.

- Groundwater levels and pore pressure behind the walls.

- Tilt and settlement of the buildings around the site.

- Vibration from piling and excavation, measured as peak particle velocity with [vibration meters](https://sentratech.in/products/vibration-meter.html) and checked against structural and comfort limits. Our guide to [construction-induced vibration monitoring](https://sentratech.in/blogs/monitoring-construction-induced-vibrations.html) explains the limits.

## Why Real Time Matters on a Metro

Metro assets are hard to reach. Inspection happens in short night-time blocks, and every hour of possession is expensive. Continuous monitoring changes what those hours are spent on:

- **Problems are found between inspections,** not at the next one, with tiered alerts by SMS, email and dashboard when a reading leaves its normal band.

- **Inspection crews are sent where the data says they are needed,** instead of working through a fixed rota.

- **Installation is faster.** Battery-powered wireless sensors need no cable runs along tunnels or viaducts, which matters when every installation visit is a night block.

- **Construction-phase data becomes the operating baseline,** so the owner inherits years of known behaviour instead of starting from zero.

Readings from tunnels, viaducts and stations can all sit on one platform, bound to the components they come from in a [digital twin](https://sentratech.in/solutions/digital-twin.html), so an alert is reported against the pier, ring or wall panel it concerns.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### What is monitored on a metro rail system?

During construction: ground settlement, tilt and cracking of buildings above tunnels, diaphragm wall deflection, groundwater and construction vibration. In operation: tunnel convergence and joint movement, viaduct pier tilt, bearing movement, dynamic response and stress at fatigue-critical details.

#### How is tunnel convergence measured?

Convergence is the change in distance across a tunnel section. It can be measured continuously with a laser distance sensor aimed at a fixed target on the opposite wall, combined with tilt sensing, so the lining is tracked without contact and without a survey crew in the tunnel.

#### Can wireless sensors work inside metro tunnels?

Yes. Wireless sensors in tunnels report over LoRaWAN through repeaters placed along the bore, which relay the signal to a gateway at a portal or shaft. Data loggers store readings locally if the link drops.
